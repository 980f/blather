Mooring (11.2.1)

Note: a mooring line under tension has a deadly amount of energy in it. (11.2.1.6)

Commands/terms (11.2.1.5,11.2.1.2)

Stand by the line
Man the line,
Get to the line, if coiled and hung on a pin lay it down but leave it belayed or tugboat hitched
Be attentive to further orders
Ease away
Pay out enough to remove most of the strain
(continue easing until told to stop)
Slack away
Pay out the line, allowing it to form an easy bight
Take in the slack
Take up all slack
Take a strain
Put the line under tension by holding back or by adding turns to the bitt
Hold the line
Take sufficient turns of the line on a bitt so that it will not give
Check the line
Hold the line, but render it enough (i.e., a foot or two at a time) when necessary to prevent parting
Double up
Run out additional lines to double all mooring lines
Single up
Take in all extra lines, leave only one in each position
Take
Give slack so that line handlers on the pier can cast off the end and bring line quickly aboard, or send ashore as needed
Cast off
Remove the line from a bitt or bollard to allow the other end to be taken in

Breast line
? These are the mooring lines that hold the ship to the dock
Spring line
These are the mooring lines that resist fore and aft motion, they are more parallel to the ship than the breast lines.
Hawse hole
The hole through the bulwark that the mooring line passes through
Dipping the line
When adding another mooring line to a bit you pass its loop up through the loops already there before putting its loop over the bitt.
This allows the lines to be removed in any order.
Rasta-haul
Rapidly get line on board.
Two crewman take turns heaving loops behind them, never both letting go, other crew drag the line out in longer loops going back for more.

To feed a mooring line from the deck out through a hawse hole:
  1. Pull about 6' of the bitter end up to the hawse hole.
  2. Pull more slack to form a large bight that you drop over the side, lined up with the hawse hole. This will take perhaps another 10 or more feet.
  3. When the bight is at the hawse hole pass the bitter end out and fully into the bight.
  4. Pull up the bight by both sides, which brings the bitter end up with it.
  5. Grab the bitter end out of the bight and drop the bight on deck.

Heaving (11.2.1.7)

  1. Tie bitter end to side of mooring line's loop, use a slippery constrictor (and don't be stingy with the bitter end of that constrictor knot, it has to get grabbed in a hurry and yanked hard).
  2. Coil line carefully in one hand making equal sized loops (so that they can't dip inside the bundle).
    Ensure that you are working out any twist in the line as you coil.
  3. Eventually step down to a smaller loop diameter for what will be in your casting hand. 
  4. Stop coiling when you can grab your preferred knot near the weight.
  5. Check your back swing and footing
  6. Yell out "Line xx Coming Over"
  7. then heave!

Naming of the lines  (11.2.1.4)

The lines are named 1 through 4 with 1 being the foremost, 4 the aftmost.
  1. Number 1 line goes through a chock on the head deck and then to the fore capstan.
  2. Number 2 goes through a hawse hole just aft of the lamp locker, then to the nearby bitts.
  3. Number 3 goes through a hawse hole just fore of the quarterdeck, then to the nearby bitts.
    It gets wrapped on the main capstan during docking.
  4. Number 4 goes through a chock on the aft of the quarterdeck, then to the nearby bitts.
The line nicknamed "anaconda" is especially strong and elastic, and is usually deployed in position ?2 or ?3. It is usually removed after all other lines are secured.
