knots

Name
some notes about it where used on Elissa
Reef / Square
two overhand knots, mirror imaged
  • gaskets on furled headsails
  • Jacob's ladder to stanchion
  • finishing step of mousing (then add buttons)
Blood
(Double Overhand)
overhand but doubled before cinching tight
  • keep line from pulling out of block or fairlead
Figure 8
over-under-over and pull tight
  • ditto Blood but for gantline where it needs to be easier to untie
Sheet Bend
wrap around bitter side of larger rope's bight first,
exit the loop over the bitter end of the bight
  • temporarily tie smaller line to heavier one to haul the heavier, such as up into rigging
Double Sheet Bend
wrap twice before leading out and tightening
  • ditto Sheet Bend but when heavier line is slick or thick
Bowline
bitter alternates over and under
double is tied in a bight including the starting loop.
  • mooring line's loop
  • fix a line to a stanchion without it locking on to stanchion
  • tool lanyards, so that cord doesn't interfere as much with use
  • main topmast staysail sheet tied to bulwark stanchion
  • [double bowline for bosun chair]
Cow Hitch
tuck ends through a bight after wrapping them around something
  • becket to jackstay
  • button for loose lines to shrouds/backstays
  • bitter end of shroud lanyards
  • [final step of gasket coil is a cow hitch]
Clove Hitch
round turn and a half hitch
  • ratlines to shrouds
Constrictor
bitter end over the standing line before finishing the clove
  • temporary whipping
  • heaving line to mooring line, make it slippery
Buntline
clove on standing,
round the outside first so that as the loop shrinks it binds on the bitter end
  • lines to edges of sail
Round Turn and 2 Half Hitches
round and loop,
clove on standing, wrap round the inside of the loop first
  • temporary hitch- such as small craft to a ring on a dock
Rolling Hitch
clove with extra wraps before crossover and final half hitch
wrap towards load,last half hitch is furthest from load.
  • stopper such as on a halyard,
    when holding so that the halyard can be belayed don't do the final half hitch but instead wrap along the lay of the heavy line and hold it to that line (so that it can be undone rapidly if the loaded line needs to be adjusted)
Rolling Hitch to self
tie to standing line, initial rounds to inside of loop
  • bitter end of gasket on furled fore-n-aft sails
  • as tent rope for awnings
Klemheist Hitch
wrap the Becket along the lay of the rope,
loop that takes load tucks into the other
  • attach jigger tackle to a line
Gasket Coil
end move is a cow hitch!
  • keep an otherwise loose line neat, esp. yardarm gasket

Mouse a shackle
figure 8's around the joint, reef with buttons to finish
  • keep the pin from coming out
Mouse a cargo hook
wrap around body,
frap the wraps in different directions to squeeze them tight,
reef with buttons to finish
  • keep the load from slipping off of the hook
Marline Hitch
constrictor, overhands around thing, constrictor
  • pull tighter on the bitter end of a line being tied
Marline Spike Hitch?
loop forward, pick up standing line with spike and pull through loop
  • add a handle to a line to be able to pull it harder
